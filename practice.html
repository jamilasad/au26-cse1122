Getting better

How to practise

You have finished beecrowd 1000–1020, so there is nothing beginner-level left there on data types and operators — and that is exactly what the retake is on. Here is how to make your own practice.

Make your own problems with free AI

Gemini and ChatGPT both have free tiers that are more than enough. Used one way they will make you much stronger. Used the other way they will destroy you in the exam room, where there is no chat window and I am watching you type.

1 · Ask for problems, never solutions

I am a first-semester C student. I have ONLY learned variables, data types, scanf and printf, the operators + - * / and %, type casting, and library functions like sqrt and pow. I have NOT learned if-else, loops, arrays or functions yet. Give me 8 practice problems I can actually solve with only that. Vary the topics. For each: a short statement, 2 sample inputs and their exact output. Do NOT give me code, and do NOT use a loop, if-else or array in any problem.

Solve all eight yourself and run them. That last line matters — without it you will be handed problems needing loops, which you have not learned, and you will think you are bad at C when you are simply not there yet.

2 · Then ask it to check, not to fix

Here is my solution and the wrong output I get for input 5 3 4. Tell me ONLY which line to look at and what idea I have misunderstood. Do not rewrite my code.

The point is that you find the bug. That is the skill the exam measures.

Good uses

  • “Explain this compile error: paste it”
  • “Why does 5/2 give 2?”
  • “Give me 3 tricky test cases for this problem”
  • “Explain %d vs %lf like I am a beginner”

These feel like learning and are not

  • “Write a program that…”
  • “Fix my code”
  • “Solve beecrowd 1017 for me”

The honest test: close everything, open a blank file, write it again from nothing. If you cannot, you watched it — you did not learn it.

If you lead a group

Group marking works one way only: nobody is lifted by the group, but the strongest are pulled down by it. Your job is not to do the work for everyone — it is to make sure nobody is left behind.

Do

  • Ask to see their screen, not their word
  • Name exact problem numbers: “1001–1020 except 1009”
  • Bring your weakest member to the repair session — their retake lifts your group average too
  • Get a strong member to explain a problem out loud. Explaining is how they learn it properly

Do not

  • Report what people tell you without checking
  • Write “all problems, all sessions” — the phrase I trust least
  • Escalate a member to me before you have genuinely tried
  • Solve it for them and let them copy. That helps nobody

Say the true number. An honest four beats a fictional six, and after a test I always find out anyway.

The habit that decides everything

One hour a day beats seven on Friday

This is a physical skill, like handwriting. It builds through daily repetition, not weekend marathons.

Type every line yourself

Never copy — not from a friend, a screen, or an AI. Reading a solution feels like learning and is not.

Stuck for 20 minutes? Ask.

Twenty minutes of being stuck is where learning happens. Three days is waste.

Keep everything you write

One folder, every program. In four years that folder is the difference between “I studied CSE” and “here is what I built”.
